# PRD Quality Review - Map Studio for Ignition Perspective

## Overall verdict

A well-built PRD for a solo project: it has a real thesis (link the map to live project data and make it quick to build), five UJs with named protagonists that each drive requirements, a strong glossary, and 33 FRs that mostly carry testable consequences. What puts it at risk is that it is not lean and has no cut line: the MVP is "Section 4 in full" against two days a week, the platform claims the standing rule says to mark unverified are stated as fact, and a few terms that carry the product's safety value (stale, comms lost, parity with the template's approved alarm map) are undefined. Fix the five high findings before Sofia and the architecture lean on it; none is critical.

## Decision-readiness - adequate

Decisions are stated as decisions (single shared Map, map store as source of truth, full Gateways only, no draft/release split), and the Open Questions (§8) are genuinely open, with the receiving step named for 4, 5 and 6. The weakness is trade-offs: the PRD says what was chosen but rarely what was given up. The clearest case is live-by-default editing. §5 lists "no draft and release split, and no version history" as a non-goal, UJ-2 celebrates "viewers see the new route and the geofence without a release step", FR-17 says "Delete asks for no confirmation", and FR-30 records only "who made it and when". Together that means a mistaken edit by any phone-editing operator is immediately visible to every viewer and cannot be reverted after the session ends. The template's current editor has drafts and an audit trail (`map_draft`, `map_draft_event`, per the addendum's follow-up), so this is a deliberate step down in safety net that the PRD never weighs.

The one `[NOTE FOR PM]` (§6.2, flow animation) is at a real tension: the initiative is named after flow and the first release defers it. That is honest and well placed. Q1 and Q2 in §8 (licence, repo rename) are housekeeping, not product questions, and add open-item count without informing a decision.

### Findings
- **medium** Live-by-default editing trade-off is not surfaced (§5, UJ-2 resolution, FR-17, FR-30) - what is given up (draft review, revert, confirmation on delete) versus the template's current draft flow is never stated, and operators on phones can write to the shared production map. *Fix:* add a short `[NOTE FOR PM]` under §5 or FR-30 naming the loss, and either accept it explicitly or add one cheap mitigation (record the previous geometry/properties in the change record so an editor can revert, FR-30).
- **low** Q1 and Q2 are not product decisions (§8) - the licence and repo rename gate nothing in this PRD. *Fix:* move them to the addendum or the open-source release backlog so §8 shows only questions that block requirements, UX or architecture.

## Substance over theater - adequate

There is no persona theater in the sense of too many personas: four roles, each with a UJ, and the operator/phone role drives FR-7, FR-27 and FR-28. The Vision is specific (Ignition 8.3, comms status plus one key value, popup link to the asset's page) and could not swap into another PRD. NFRs are mostly bounded (WCAG 2.5.7/2.5.8/1.4.11 citations, 44 px, 390 px, 3:1 contrast), with one exception: NFR-1 (see Done-ness). The Integrator role is the weak spot: §2.1 defines it as "everything a map editor does, plus mass provisioning by CSV import", but FR-22, FR-23 and FR-24 grant import to "a map editor", and UJ-1 has Sam, a map editor, importing a file. No FR distinguishes the Integrator, so the role is furniture unless a permission actually differs.

### Findings
- **medium** Integrator role has no distinct requirement (§2.1, FR-23, UJ-1 step 5, UJ-3) - CSV import is granted to the map editor in the FRs, so the "mass provisioning" distinction in §2.1 is not testable. *Fix:* either make import an integrator-only permission in FR-7 or fold Integrator into Map editor and keep Jordan as a map editor with a bulk-provisioning journey.

## Strategic coherence - adequate

The thesis is clear and stated once, in the Vision's last line: "a map that is quick to build and stays connected to live values". The features follow it (kind setup written once, asset links, import, popups with live values), and SM-2 (30-minute build) and SM-5 (alarm to page in two clicks) test the two halves. Counter-metrics exist (SM-C1, SM-C2). The MVP kind is a platform/tool replacement scope, honestly described.

The problems are scope and parity. §6.1 puts "Section 4 in full" in the MVP, which is 33 FRs including offline place check (bundled boundary and city data, FR-21), bulk edit, reference layers, line split, snapping, undo/redo of every edit, two-editor conflict handling, phone editing and search, for a solo owner at two days a week and a target of end of January 2027 (about 14 weeks). Nothing marks which FRs are the thesis and which are nice to have, so when the date slips, the first cut will be made under pressure. Second, SM-1 ("It replaces the template's map pages") is the first primary metric, yet the PRD never states what parity means. The template's current map has an owner-approved alarm presentation (ER-27, `docs/ux.md`): IEC shapes with the priority digit, dimming of off-scope sites, pipeline segments that take alarm and comms state from their taps, and a flow arrow on the selected segment. The PRD's FR-2 gives lines only "their kind's style" and has no segment state, so retiring the template's map could remove behaviour operators now rely on.

### Findings
- **high** MVP has no cut line against capacity (§6.1 "Section 4 in full", §7 target date) - 33 FRs and a ~14-week window at two days a week; no ordering of what carries the thesis. *Fix:* tier the FRs in §6.1 into "thesis core" (FR-1, 2, 4 to 6, 7, 9 to 14, 20, 21, 29, 30), "MVP if time" and "first after MVP". Place check (FR-21), bulk edit (FR-25), reference layer (FR-26), line split and FR-31 are the natural candidates to tier below core.
- **high** SM-1 parity with the template's approved map is undefined (§7 SM-1, FR-2, §6.1 last bullet; template `docs/ux.md` ER-27) - the PRD does not say whether ER-27's shapes, digit labels, scope dimming, segment alarm state and selected-segment flow arrow carry over, are dropped, or are re-reviewed. FR-2 gives lines no state at all. *Fix:* add a parity statement to SM-1 listing what the template map does today that Map Studio must match (or explicitly drops), and a line-state requirement or a `[NON-GOAL for MVP]` for segment state. Note that §4.2 already says ER-27 review applies before release; say which behaviours it will review.
- **medium** SM-2 has no baseline and no tester (§7 SM-2) - "an admin who has not seen the Mesa demo field" is hard to find for a solo project, and without today's time to build the same field with the template's tools, 30 minutes cannot show the "quick to build" claim. *Fix:* record the current time (owner's own run in the template editor) as the baseline, and name who runs the test.

## Done-ness clarity - adequate

This is the PRD's strongest structural feature: nearly every FR has a "Consequences (testable)" list, usually with concrete conditions (FR-6's `/ctb/{asset.id}` goes to `/ctb/CTB1`, FR-14's finishing gestures, FR-17's keys). The gaps cluster in three places.

The term Stale is the foundation of the safety-relevant behaviour. §3 defines it as "a value whose source has not updated in time, or whose comms are lost", and FR-2, FR-4 and UJ-5 depend on it, but "in time" has no threshold, no owner of the threshold (kind setup? project?), and no rule for deriving comms state from the "comms path" tag (boolean, quality, heartbeat age?). A tester cannot write the stale test, and a viewer's trust in the icon rests on it.

NFR-1 is the PRD's one adjective NFR: "stay smooth", "an edit shows on the map at once" are not bounds. The basis, "the Mesa demo field", is also thin: the template's `assets/fields/Mesa/pads.csv` has six pads, while UJ-3 provisions a 120-pad field and §6.2 defers "Scale beyond the demo field (300 sites, 2,000 segments)". The MVP therefore has no performance bar at the size UJ-3 itself describes, and nothing on how many live-bound icons the page may hold.

Several FRs have consequences that do not test the requirement they sit under. FR-20 says the form "remembers the last entry per kind" but its only consequence is about required fields. FR-28's single consequence ("Drawing tools are not offered below a set screen width") tests neither the bottom sheet nor property editing, and "a set screen width" is unset. FR-3's second consequence is a configuration option, not a verifiable behaviour. FR-10 "say clearly when it is empty" and FR-19 "plain words" are adjectives.

### Findings
- **high** "Stale" and comms state have no rule (§3 Stale, FR-2, FR-4, UJ-5 edge case) - "has not updated in time" is unquantified and the mapping from the comms path tag to "Comms lost" is unstated. *Fix:* define in §3 how comms state is derived (value or quality of the comms path) and how stale is determined (an age threshold, who sets it, default), and add a consequence to FR-2 that tests it. Mark the Perspective mechanism as unverified (see the claims finding below).
- **high** NFR-1 is unmeasurable and its basis is six pads (§4.11 NFR-1; template `assets/fields/Mesa/pads.csv`; UJ-3 "120-pad field") - "smooth" and "at once" cannot be tested, and the demo field is far smaller than UJ-3. *Fix:* give numbers: a frame-rate or interaction latency bound, a stated item count (for example "120 pads, N segments, M live-bound icons on a laptop and a named phone class"), and an edit-visible time. Keep 300 sites as the later target.
- **medium** FR-2 leaves the icon KPI and comms semantics open (FR-2, FR-12) - the kind setup holds "up to 4" KPIs, FR-2 shows "one KPI" on the icon, and nothing says which one. *Fix:* add to FR-12 a flag naming the icon KPI (for example the first) and a consequence in FR-2 that checks it.
- **medium** Several FRs' consequences do not test the requirement (FR-20 "remembers the last entry", FR-28 "a set screen width", FR-3 second bullet, FR-10, FR-19) - story acceptance criteria will have to invent the tests. *Fix:* add one verifiable consequence per FR (FR-20: reopening the form for the same kind prefills the last entry; FR-28: set the breakpoint, for example 768 px, and test that the sheet edits a property and saves it).
- **medium** Import commit and matching rules are ambiguous (FR-23, FR-24, FR-29, FR-11, UJ-3 steps 2 and 3) - FR-23 says imported items appear "before anything is saved" and can be cancelled, while FR-29 says "every finished edit is saved without a save button"; it is not stated when the import commits. FR-24 matches "assets with the same name", but FR-11 permits shared names across items, so exact versus case-insensitive match and duplicate handling are undefined. *Fix:* state that an import is one edit committed on an explicit Apply, and give the match rule (exact, case-insensitive, how ties and duplicates are reported).
- **medium** Undo scope and conflict interplay are unspecified (FR-17, FR-29, FR-31) - undo of an edit that is already saved (a new write, an overwrite of another editor's change?), how deep, and whether history survives reload are not said; FR-31 refuses the second save but does not say what happens to the refused user's edit. *Fix:* add consequences: undo is per session, after a refused save the user's edit is kept visible for retry, bulk edit conflicts refuse per batch or per item.
- **low** UJ-5 climax says "within two clicks of seeing the alarm" but the path includes a third click (acknowledging in the table); the resolution and SM-5 count only two (§2.3 UJ-5, SM-5). *Fix:* say that acknowledging is optional and not counted.

## Scope honesty - adequate

§5 and §6.2 do real work, with an honest deferral of the idea's original purpose, an explicit list of what MVP leaves out, and a clean boundary on writing (no tags, devices or assets). The ledgers are thin elsewhere. Section 9 reads "None open. Every assumption made during discovery was confirmed by the owner and is recorded in `.memlog.md`", which is neither an index a downstream reader can audit nor consistent with the content: the PRD has no inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags, yet many figures are picks, and it states Perspective and module-SDK behaviour as fact. The standing rule for this project is that every claim about the Perspective client API or module SDK stays marked unverified until checked on a live Gateway. NFR-7 states the rule in general, but individual claims are not tagged. Candidates with no mark: FR-5 (the built-in Alarm Status Table can be embedded in a popup of a module component and filtered to an alarms path, and acknowledging "follows Ignition's own permissions"); FR-6 ("An outside URL opens as Perspective opens outside URLs"); FR-7 (the component can read the user's roles, and the Gateway can check them on a save); FR-30 ("Map Studio creates and upgrades its own tables" through the named connection); FR-32 (the token comes from the project's secret store to the browser); NFR-4 ("host project's theme tokens" reachable from a module component); FR-27 (long-press and drag on touch in a Perspective component). Only Q6 (alarm aggregation) and the addendum's "unverified" notes are labelled.

Gaps that a reader would infer silently: what happens to links and items when a linked asset is removed, renamed or gets a new base path, or when a kind is removed from the project's list while items of that kind exist (§4.4 covers adding kinds only); what viewers see when the map store is unreachable (FR-29 covers failed saves, not failed loads, yet the map store is the only source of the map); and whether a phone-editing operator on a desktop is allowed to edit (FR-7 defines two roles by name, FR-28 ties phone editing to a phone).

### Findings
- **high** Platform claims are stated as fact, not marked unverified (FR-5, FR-6, FR-7, FR-27, FR-30, FR-32, NFR-4; §9) - violates the standing rule; NFR-7 is a blanket statement and §9 declares nothing open. *Fix:* tag each claim inline with `[UNVERIFIED: Perspective/SDK, check on live 8.3 Gateway]` (or an `[ASSUMPTION]` tag), and list them in §9 as the checklist for the spike. Group them: alarm table embedding and filter (FR-5), roles and Gateway check (FR-7), module-created tables (FR-30), secrets to client (FR-32), theme tokens (NFR-4), touch events (FR-27).
- **medium** Orphan and failure states are unspecified (§4.4, §4.9, FR-11, FR-29) - linked asset removed or renamed, kind removed, base path changed, map store unreachable on load. *Fix:* add one FR or consequence each: orphaned items show as unlinked with a reason; items of an unknown kind stay visible with a neutral generic icon; on load failure, the component shows an error state in words, never an empty map that looks like "no sites".
- **low** Assumptions are recorded outside the PRD (§9, `.memlog.md`) - a downstream reader cannot see which figures were chosen versus confirmed. *Fix:* index the real assumptions in §9 (4 KPIs, 44 px and 390 px, 30 minutes, "a few seconds", offline place check feasibility) in a line each, or state that they are owner-confirmed in-line.
- **low** NFR-5 says Map Studio "writes only to its map store" while UJ-5 and FR-5 let the viewer acknowledge alarms through the embedded table (§4.11 NFR-5, §2.3 UJ-5) - the write is Ignition's, not Map Studio's, but a reader will notice. *Fix:* add "apart from alarm acknowledgement, which the built-in alarm table performs under Ignition's own permissions".

## Downstream usability - strong

The Glossary is thorough and mostly used exactly; UJs carry named protagonists (Sam, Jordan, Alex, Riley) with context inline; every FR cites a UJ; UJ and SM cross-references resolve; IDs are unique. Sofia, UX and the architecture can source-extract from this cleanly. The remaining issues are mechanical and are listed below. The brownfield references checked (ER-27 and `docs/ux.md` §11, `prov` map steps, the Mesa field) exist in the template.

### Findings
- **low** Term drift (§2.1, §2.3, §4, §7) - "admin" is used in the Vision, UJ-1 and SM-2 but is not a defined role (the roles are map editor, integrator, operator, viewer); "item" is used for "map item" in several places ("every item is linked", "An item with comms lost"); "Mesa demo field" is used in NFR-1 and SM-2 but is not in the Glossary and exists only in the template. *Fix:* add "admin" to the Roles line as a way to hold the map editor role, use "map item" consistently, and add "Mesa demo field" to the Glossary with its size.

## Shape fit - adequate

For a tool that a module author builds, a map-editing product with distinct roles and a UX specification to follow, UJs with named protagonists are load-bearing, and the PRD's brownfield framing (replace the template's map; follow-up pull request in the addendum) is clear and accurate. It is over-formalised for a solo owner in one respect: it carries interaction design detail that belongs in the UX specification and pre-empts it, while Q4 asks that same UX spec where the tools live. Examples: "a red underline and a tooltip", "the pin is drawn above Alex's finger", an "Undo bar", the `?` key, Ctrl+Z/Ctrl+Shift+Z, "Esc returns to Select". The interaction principles are good; stating them as FR consequences makes the UX step confirm rather than design.

### Findings
- **low** UX detail pre-empts the UX specification (FR-8, FR-11, FR-17, FR-18, FR-27; UJ-1 and UJ-4 edge cases; vs Q4 in §8) - a pin drawn above the finger and a red underline are design decisions presented as requirements. *Fix:* keep the user-visible outcome ("the point stays visible while dragged", "a duplicate name is flagged") and move the specific treatments to the addendum as input to the UX specification.

## Mechanical notes

- **Numbering:** §2 jumps from 2.1 to 2.3 (no 2.2). FR-33 (Search and zoom) sits in §4.1 after FR-3, out of sequence; the IDs FR-1 to FR-33 are otherwise contiguous and unique. SM-1 "Validates FR-1 to FR-32" omits FR-33, and no SM covers FR-33.
- **Section 4.1 description** says "Realizes UJ-2, UJ-3, UJ-5", but the FRs in 4.1 all cite only UJ-5.
- **ID cross-references:** UJ-1 to UJ-5, SM-1 to SM-5, SM-C1 and SM-C2, NFR-1 to NFR-8 resolve; the "(from 2.1)" and "Section 3" and "section 9" references resolve.
- **Assumptions Index roundtrip:** nothing to round-trip - there are no inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags and §9 is empty (see Scope honesty).
- **UJ protagonists:** all five are named (Sam appears twice, as a map editor in UJ-1 and UJ-2).
- **SM-C2** ("a normal field should look mostly grey") is not measurable as written; low-weight on a counter-metric, but a count of non-neutral items in normal state would make it one.
- **Glossary:** the Roles line repeats §2.1 by reference; "Kind setup" versus "kind's setup" and "tools" versus "Map Studio tools" drift lightly.
- **Em dashes:** none found in the PRD or addendum.
